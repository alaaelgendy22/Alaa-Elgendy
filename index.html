⚠️ The Problem

In nuclear PWR reactors, a Loss-of-Coolant Accident (LOCA) is one of the most critical emergency scenarios. Operators need immediate diagnosis of break severity — but traditional methods require complex simulations that take time. The challenge: can deep learning classify break size in real time from sensor data alone?

🗂️ Dataset

Simulation-generated time-series data from the WSC (Westinghouse Simulator) covering 13,000+ reactor operational records. Parameters included: Pressure, Temperature, Thermal Power, Coolant Flow, and Boron Concentration — all evolving over time across 5 LOCA break-size scenarios.

🔬 Why LSTM?

Reactor parameters are sequential — a pressure spike at T+0 has a completely different diagnostic meaning than the same spike at T+120 seconds. LSTM networks carry memory through time, capturing these temporal dependencies in exactly the same way an experienced engineer reads trend charts over time.

⚙️ Approach & Methodology
01
Data Preparation — Cleaned and normalized WSC simulator output. Applied IQR outlier capping on sensor readings. Constructed sliding-window sequences for LSTM input.
02
Exploratory Analysis — Identified discriminative signal windows where parameters diverge most strongly between severity classes. Domain knowledge guided feature selection over purely statistical methods.
03
LSTM Architecture — Multi-layer LSTM with dropout regularization. Systematic grid search over units, sequence length, learning rate, and batch size.
04
Validation — Evaluated with MSE, RMSE, and R². Applied ±15% engineering tolerance framework standard in reactor safety analysis.
📊 Results

Achieved 80.68% classification accuracy across 5 LOCA break-size classes within the ±15% engineering tolerance. Results demonstrated the viability of deep learning for safety-critical sequential classification and showed that AI can support — not replace — reactor engineers by providing rapid first-pass severity estimates.

💡 Key Lessons
⚛️ Domain knowledge is a superpower — understanding the physics made feature selection obvious where brute-force statistics would have failed.
📈 Time-series preprocessing (window size, stride, normalization) mattered more than model architecture choices.
🎯 Safety-critical accuracy is judged differently — 80.68% with engineering tolerance is publishable; without that framework it's just a number.

A controlled ML benchmark comparing 5 algorithms on 13,393 physiological records — KNN, Linear Regression, Decision Tree, SVM, and Neural Network — with SHAP & LIME explainability. SVM achieved 75% accuracy with the best bias-variance trade-off.

🔬 The Research Question

Not "build a model and report accuracy." This was a controlled scientific experiment: compare 5 fundamentally different ML algorithms under identical conditions — same dataset, same train/test splits, same evaluation framework — to understand which approach best suits physiological performance prediction and why.

🗂️ Dataset & EDA

13,393 records with 12 feature columns covering VO2 max, heart rate variability, sleep quality, body composition, and training load. Zero missing values after preprocessing. IQR-based outlier capping on 3.2% of extreme readings. Correlation heatmap revealed strong r=0.71 between VO2 max and performance — and multicollinearity between recovery metrics that informed feature selection.

⚖️ Algorithm Comparison

🔮 SHAP & LIME Explainability

Beyond accuracy — applied SHAP (global feature importance) and LIME (local prediction explanations) to the best model. SHAP revealed VO2 max and recovery index as the top two drivers — consistent with sports science domain knowledge and confirming the model learned real signal, not spurious correlation. LIME confirmed consistent decision logic in the feature neighborhood of each individual prediction.

💡 Key Lessons
🔬 Comparing algorithms reveals what a single model cannot — the 14-point KNN accuracy cliff with unscaled data taught more than any textbook.
⚖️ Decision Tree's 94% train / 71% test gap is a live demonstration of overfitting — no synthetic example can replicate seeing it on real data.
🎯 SHAP validation is essential — without it, 75% accuracy is just a number; with it, it becomes a finding backed by domain evidence.
